How to Make a Home Inventory for Insurance (Step-by-Step Checklist)

If a storm, fire or break-in damages your home, your insurance company will ask what you owned. A home inventory made ahead of time makes that question easy to answer.

Updated November 2026 · By the Nestory team

Short answer: walk through your home room by room, take photos of everything of value, write down the brand, model, serial number and approximate purchase date and price of larger items, keep receipts where you can, and store the inventory somewhere other than your house, such as a cloud account. Update it once a year and after big purchases.

Why a home inventory matters

After a loss, most insurers ask you to list what was damaged or destroyed, often with details like brand, age and value. Doing that from memory, in a stressful moment, is hard. People commonly forget a large share of what they owned. A list made before anything happens helps you file a more complete claim, and it also helps you check whether you have enough coverage in the first place.

Step-by-step: how to make your home inventory

  1. Start with the big things. Appliances, electronics, furniture, and home systems like the water heater, furnace and AC are the most expensive to replace.
  2. Go room by room. Kitchen, living room, bedrooms, bathrooms, garage, basement, attic and outdoor areas. Don't forget closets and storage.
  3. Take photos. One wide photo of each room, then close-ups of valuable items. A slow video walk-through works too.
  4. Photograph the labels. The sticker with the model and serial number is the fastest way to prove exactly what you had.
  5. Add the details. Brand, model, serial number, approximate purchase date and price. Exact numbers aren't required for everything; good estimates are far better than nothing.
  6. Keep receipts and warranties. A photo of the receipt is enough. Warranties also matter after smaller incidents, like an appliance that fails on its own.
  7. Store it off-site. A list kept only in a drawer, or only on a computer at home, can be lost in the same event. Save it to a cloud account and keep a copy you can share.
  8. Update it once a year. Add big purchases as you make them, and do a quick review each year, for example at the start of storm season.

What to include

  • Major appliances and home systems (with model and serial numbers)
  • TVs, computers, phones and other electronics
  • Furniture and rugs
  • Jewelry, art and collectibles (these may need separate coverage, so ask your insurer)
  • Tools, bikes and outdoor equipment
  • Clothing and shoes (a photo of each closet is usually enough)
  • Repair and renovation records, which show the condition and upgrades of your home

If something happens

Every policy is different, so always follow your insurer's instructions. Common advice includes:

  • Contact your insurance company as soon as you safely can.
  • Take photos and video of the damage before cleaning up, and prevent further damage if it's safe to do so.
  • Don't throw out damaged items until your insurer says it's OK.
  • Keep receipts for repairs and for any extra living costs if you can't stay in your home.
  • Share your inventory list with your adjuster.

This guide is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Check your policy or talk to your agent about what's covered.
