Where to Find the Model and Serial Number on Any Appliance

Every repair, warranty claim and replacement part starts with the same question: what's the model number? Here's where to find it on each appliance.

Updated November 2026 · By the Nestory team

Short answer: the model and serial number are printed on a sticker or metal plate, usually somewhere you can see with the door open: on the inside wall or ceiling of a refrigerator, the edge of a dishwasher door, the door frame of a washer or dryer, behind the storage drawer of an oven, and on the side of a water heater tank.

Where to look, appliance by appliance

ApplianceWhere the label usually is
RefrigeratorInside the fresh food section on a side wall or the ceiling, near the crisper drawers, or at the bottom behind the front grille. Some are on the back.
Chest freezerOn the back or side of the outside cabinet, or under the lid.
DishwasherOpen the door and look along the edge of the door or the side of the tub opening.
Front-load washerAround the door opening, inside the door frame, or on the back panel.
Top-load washerUnder the lid, on the back of the control panel, or on the back of the cabinet.
DryerInside the door opening on the frame, or on the back panel.
Range or ovenBehind the storage or warming drawer, on the frame behind the oven door, or on the back.
Over-the-range microwaveInside the door frame or on the inside wall when the door is open.
Countertop microwaveOn the back or the bottom.
Water heaterOn the rating plate, a large label on the side of the tank.
FurnaceInside the front access panel, on the blower compartment door or wall.
Central AC (outdoor unit)On the data plate on the side or back of the condenser.
Garbage disposalOn the bottom of the unit, under the sink.

Locations vary by brand and model. If you can't find it, the manual or the manufacturer's website usually shows exactly where to look.

Model number vs. serial number

The model number tells you what kind of appliance it is. You need it for manuals, replacement parts and repair quotes. The serial number identifies your specific unit. You need it for warranty claims and recalls, and many brands encode the manufacture date in it, which tells you how old the appliance is.

Tips

  • Take a photo of every label now, while nothing is broken. It takes a few minutes for the whole house.
  • Use your phone's flashlight and zoom for labels in dark corners.
  • If a label is worn, check the original box, receipt or registration email.
  • Register new appliances with the manufacturer. It can unlock longer warranties and recall notices.
