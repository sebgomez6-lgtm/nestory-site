How Long Do Appliances Last? Typical Lifespans for Every Major Appliance

Most major appliances last 8 to 20 years. Here's what to expect from each one, the signs one is near the end, and how to plan for it before it breaks.

Updated November 2026 · By the Nestory team

Short answer: a refrigerator typically lasts 10–15 years, a washer 10–14, a dishwasher 9–12, a tank water heater 8–12, a furnace 15–20 and a central air conditioner 12–17. How you use and maintain an appliance can move those numbers by several years in either direction.

These are typical ranges gathered from manufacturer guidance and home industry estimates. Your appliance may last more or less depending on the brand, how often it runs, your water quality and how well it's maintained.

Signs an appliance is near the end

  • Refrigerator: food spoils faster, the motor runs constantly, frost builds up, or the back gets unusually hot.
  • Washer and dryer: loud banging, leaks, clothes still wet or not drying, or the drum won't spin.
  • Dishwasher: dishes come out dirty, water pools in the bottom, or rust appears inside the tub.
  • Water heater: rusty or cloudy hot water, rumbling noises, a leak around the base, or hot water running out quickly.
  • Furnace and AC: uneven temperatures, short cycling, rising energy bills, or repairs every season.

Repair or replace?

A common rule of thumb: if an appliance is past half of its expected lifespan and the repair would cost more than half the price of a new one, replacing it usually makes more sense. Before you pay for any repair, check whether the part is still under warranty. Compressors, sealed systems, heat exchangers and water heater tanks often have longer coverage than the rest of the appliance.

How to make appliances last longer

  • Change your HVAC filter every 1–3 months.
  • Vacuum the refrigerator's condenser coils once a year.
  • Clean the dishwasher filter every month.
  • Clean the dryer lint trap after every load and the dryer vent once a year.
  • Flush the water heater once a year to clear sediment.
  • Have the furnace serviced in the fall and the AC in the spring.

The simple habit that helps most: write down install dates

Most people have no idea how old their water heater or furnace is until it fails. If you record the install or purchase date of each appliance, you'll know which ones are getting close to the end and can budget for them instead of being surprised by an emergency replacement. The model and serial number usually encode the manufacture date too, so save those while you're at it.
