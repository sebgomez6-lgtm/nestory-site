What's Under Warranty in Your Home? A Homeowner's Guide

Many homeowners pay for repairs that a warranty would have covered. Here's what is often still under warranty in your home, and how to find out.

Updated November 2026 · By the Nestory team

Short answer: appliances usually come with a 1-year manufacturer warranty, but key parts often last longer: refrigerator compressors frequently 5–10 years, water heater tanks often 6–12 years, and HVAC parts commonly 5–10 years when registered. Roof shingles and windows often carry 10–25+ year manufacturer warranties, and new construction homes usually have a builder warranty.

The three kinds of warranties in a home

1. Manufacturer warranties

These come with each product, from appliances to shingles to windows. They cover defects, not wear and tear or damage. Coverage often differs by part: the whole appliance might be covered for one year, while the most expensive part is covered much longer.

2. Builder warranties (new construction)

Many builders offer a warranty on a newly built home. A common structure is 1 year for workmanship and materials, 2 years for systems like plumbing, electrical and HVAC, and 10 years for major structural defects. Terms vary by builder and state, so read yours.

3. Home warranties (service contracts)

A home warranty is a separate service plan, often bought by the seller or buyer at closing, that helps pay for repairs to systems and appliances, usually for one year at a time with a service fee per visit. It's different from a manufacturer warranty, and the two can overlap.

Typical coverage by item

ItemTypical manufacturer coverage
Most appliances1 year parts and labor
Refrigerator compressor / sealed systemOften 5–10 years on parts
Water heater tankOften 6–12 years, depending on the model
Furnace, AC and heat pump partsOften 5–10 years, with the longer term usually requiring registration
Furnace heat exchangerOften 20 years to lifetime
Roof shinglesOften 25 years to "limited lifetime" on materials. Installer workmanship is separate.
WindowsOften 10–20 years, some lifetime on the glass

These are common ranges, not guarantees. Check the warranty for your exact model.

Don't miss these

  • Registration deadlines. Some HVAC brands only give the longer parts warranty if the system is registered within a set time after installation, often about 60 days.
  • Transfers. Some roof and window warranties transfer to a new owner, sometimes only if you register the transfer. If you just bought a home, ask the seller for warranty documents.
  • Credit card protection. Some credit cards extend the manufacturer's warranty on items you bought with them. Benefits vary and change, so check your card's terms.
  • Proof of purchase. Most claims need the purchase or install date. Keep receipts and installer invoices.

How to find what's still covered

  1. List your appliances and systems, with model and serial numbers.
  2. Look up each warranty on the manufacturer's website using the model number.
  3. Write down each end date, especially for the expensive parts.
  4. Set a reminder a month or two before each one expires, so you can have anything suspicious checked while it's still free.
